# Boston: A Seven-Day Trip

The last line of my previous [Bay Area travel log](https://luohy15.com/bay-area-2025) was: "Hopefully next time I can explore the East Coast, places like New York and Boston."

A bit over a year later, I actually went. Early September 2026.

This trip was part work, part travel. I have been working remotely for my current company for over a year and had never met a single colleague in person. This was the trip to fix that.

It only happened because of my classmate L. He was coming here for a conference, and I tagged along. If he had not come, I probably would not have either.

Again, this is the travel log.

## Day -1: Before the Trip

I made exactly one decision before leaving: no laptop.

One week away, and a laptop is too heavy. So I brought the iPad and the iPhone only. Partly as an experiment, to see whether a tablet alone is enough to get work done.

Three things I wanted out of the trip:

- Meet my colleagues, especially my boss
- Walden Pond. I brought the book specifically to reread it there
- What an ordinary week here actually feels like

## Day 0: Three Legs

Left Hangzhou West in the morning Beijing time, to Hongqiao, then the intercity train to Pudong to wait for the flight.

Started adjusting to the time zone as soon as I boarded at noon. The guy next to me worked in investing. We talked for a while and exchanged WeChat.

Transferred in San Francisco, where I ran into a retired couple traveling around the world in the terminal. I envy that state of life.

The time difference had not caught up with me yet, so I nearly fell asleep waiting at the gate, then slept hard the moment I got on the plane. The plains and desert across the American West had great weather that day. I did not look at any of it. I slept the whole way to Boston.

Landed in the evening local time with L. The Silver Line from the airport into the city is free. Got to the hotel past one in the morning and went straight to sleep.

## Day 1: Seaport Feels Empty

I meant to get breakfast, but I woke up late and skipped it.

Lunch was udon in Seaport, at Yume Ga Arukara. Pretty good.

Afterwards I walked around the harbor area.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/congress-street.jpg)

In the afternoon I went to Boston Common, the Freedom Trail, and Quincy Market. It rained, so I looked around briefly and headed back.

For dinner I found a Chinese noodle place and ordered tomato and egg noodles. Way too salty. Boston's seasoning is still too heavy for me.

After dinner I walked along the water again.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/seaport-harborwalk.jpg)

My strongest impression on day one: where is everybody? It feels so empty.

I figured it out later. This is Seaport. Offices and convention space built on landfill. Office workers during the day, no street life at night, plus a day of rain on top. The life of this city is somewhere else, and you have to go looking for it.

## Day 2: MIT

Met two colleagues to walk around MIT.

In the morning we went to Tatte Bakery and Cafe, croissant and matcha. Lunch was Legal Sea Foods, expensive.

In the afternoon they showed me around, both the academic and residential sides, in a lot of detail. Took the obligatory tourist photo at the Great Dome.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/mit-great-dome.jpg)

Then they took me to the dining hall.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/charles-river-from-mit.jpg)

There was a dragon boat parked by the river with "Hangzhou, Living Poetry" written on the hull.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/hangzhou-dragon-boat.jpg)

Flew ten thousand kilometers from Hangzhou and found Hangzhou on the Charles River.

In the evening I met a former colleague and talked for a bit. Seriously impressive person.

That was the fullest day of the trip.

## Day 3: Walden Pond

This was supposed to be the whale watching day, but the forecast called for rain, so I dropped it and had udon again instead.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/boston-tea-party-ships.jpg)

I looked it up and found the Commuter Rail goes to Walden Pond. So that is where we went.

Got off at Concord.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/concord-road.jpg)

The suburbs here are a completely different experience. Housing in the community we visited is probably expensive, which is likely why it is so clean, quiet, and tidy. Every single house is well maintained.

It was a thirty to forty minute walk from the station to the pond, and about an hour and a half to loop around it.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/walden-pond.jpg)

By the time we finished the loop the sky had cleared up. New England in the fall is genuinely comfortable.

There is a marker at the original site of Thoreau's cabin:

> I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach, and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/thoreau-marker.jpg)

On the way back we got off at Harvard for a look. I did not rub the statue, just took a photo with it.

Then we walked to Market Basket to buy lobster, way cheaper than Legal Sea Foods.

Though you could smell weed on the walk over. All I can say is, it is not as comfortable as the small town.

## Day 4: An Omakase, Then Moving Hotels

L flew back this day, so it was just me from here on.

Went to a colleague's place for a meal.

He had gotten up early to prep, and made omakase: ankimo (monkfish liver), tuna, salmon, shrimp, crab, sea urchin.

I have never had private-chef treatment at that level before. Really something.

Afterwards he drove me to the station. The weather was great that day.

In the afternoon I met a senior from my university who works here, and we talked about life in Boston. Sounds pretty good overall.

For dinner I found a place called Pad Thai and finally got rice. Rice suits my stomach better.

This was the day I moved to the second hotel, in Back Bay. I booked a high floor, and the view was excellent. Loved it.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/back-bay-window.jpg)

There is a bubble tea shop downstairs, so I got a fruit tea. Very comfortable.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/fruit-tea-sunset.jpg)

## Day 5: Duck Tour

Met my boss and his wife, and we did the Boston Duck Tour together.

The vehicle is amphibious. The whole thing takes 80 minutes: about an hour on land, then it drives straight down a ramp next to the Museum of Science into the Charles River and floats around for another 20 minutes.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/charles-from-back-bay.jpg)

Whenever we passed another duck boat, the guide had the whole vehicle quack together, and people on the street quacked back.

I honestly did not catch much of the English narration at the time. I went back and read up on it afterwards.

The weather was excellent and so was the whole experience. We took a photo together outside the New England Aquarium at the end.

Over a year into the job, and this was the first time meeting my boss face to face. Lucky to have had the chance.

## Day 6: Chipotle

Spent the day pushing work forward. Cannot just play the whole time.

In the evening I met another colleague, talked about how my week had been, and took a photo together.

Chipotle came up while we were talking, so we went and ate there. I also showed him the Chinese Chipotle menu page I built for myself.

The conversation went all over the place: Chinese EVs, how each of us uses AI (I introduced my own setup, y-agent), environmental protection versus economic growth, Michelin restaurants, travel, and how much China has developed. At the end I told him to look me up if he ever gets the chance to visit.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/back-bay-night.jpg)

## Day 7: Last Full Day

Still working, nothing scheduled. Just walked around Back Bay and picked up some souvenirs.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/newbury-street.jpg)

## On Food

When you are out alone, food exposes you completely.

Things I definitely ate this week: udon twice, Tatte, the MIT dining hall, a Chinese noodle place, Legal Sea Foods, omakase at a colleague's place, Pad Thai, Chipotle.

Legal Sea Foods was very expensive, and the omakase was someone else cooking. Of the meals I actually paid for and chose myself, nearly all landed on cheap Southeast Asian and Mexican food.

Here is even better evidence. This week I used my own tooling to translate the menus of Pad Thai, Pho Basil, Chipotle, Legal Sea Foods, and Fogo de Chao into Chinese pages, and turned the process into a reusable skill along the way. I translated the whole set. I still only ate at the cheap ones.

So was I touring America or touring Southeast Asia? Although my agent pointed out that America is a country of immigrants, so this is perfectly normal.

Thinking about it, I am just not willing to spend much, and especially not when I am on my own.

## Day 8: Going Home

Early flight, BOS to SFO to PVG.

![](https://cdn.luohy15.com/images/boston/united-bos-sunrise.jpg)

## Postscript

The iPad-instead-of-laptop experiment: verdict is that it is enough. For a trip of about a week, that setup never gave me trouble.

The Bay Area trip was a friend showing me around. This time I put it together myself, and over the week turned people I had been talking to online for a year into real people. That is the biggest difference between the two trips, much bigger than the difference between the coasts.

That line at the end of the last post about going to the East Coast came true. No idea where I want to go next.
