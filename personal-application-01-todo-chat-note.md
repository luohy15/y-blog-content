# Personal Application 01: Todo, Chat, and Note, the Foundation

I'm starting a Personal Application series: short descriptions of the personal apps I use. No code, just what each app handles and how information moves between them. Writing this down also helps me work out what belongs together and what doesn't.

Take writing a blog post. It might begin with an idea in a conversation, followed by a few reference articles, an outline, a first draft, and revisions. When I come back a few days later, I need more than an answer to "Is it done?" I need to know what the post is meant to cover, where the draft is, and what we decided last time.

I use Todo, Chat, and Note to keep these kinds of information separate but connected. They are the three building blocks of my personal apps.

## Start in Chat and work out what to do

When I have an idea, I usually start by talking to an agent. I don't necessarily know what the result should be yet. There may just be a question worth thinking through.

For a post about personal apps, for example, we first discuss who it's for, what to cover, and which examples to use. Once the direction is clear, I ask the agent to organize an outline and write a draft, then give feedback.

So Chat isn't just a place to store conversation history. It's where we discuss and move the work forward. But talking about something doesn't mean it's done, and a longer conversation doesn't necessarily mean a clearer direction.

## Put work that needs follow-through in Todo

Once I decide to turn that idea into a post, it needs a Todo.

The title says what to do. The description defines the scope, such as "Explain how the basic modules divide the work, without code." Priority says what comes first; status says whether the work has started or finished. A progress update leaves enough to pick it up again: "First draft ready; simplify the examples next."

One post can involve more than one conversation. Choosing the topic, outlining, and revising can happen in different Chats, all linked to the same Todo. A new chat window doesn't make it a new task, and I shouldn't have to guess how far along it is.

Todo doesn't need to hold the entire discussion or draft. It gives me a quick answer to three questions: **What needs doing, where does it stand, and what's next?**

## Keep the content in Note

The outline and draft need a stable home. Scrolling back through messages to find "the latest version" isn't enough. I put that content in Note and link it to the Todo.

In Chat, we might propose three directions and reject two. Note keeps the direction we've chosen, the reasoning worth preserving, and the latest draft. If we change our minds later, we update that content rather than leaving whoever picks up the work next to infer the decision from the conversation.

A Todo can link to several Notes, such as a plan and a draft. An app description can also be shared by several tasks, without copying the background every time. The documents remain useful after a task is finished.

For outside articles I refer to while writing, Link can preserve the source. In Note, I record what's worth drawing on and my own understanding. The distinction is simple: **Saving a link preserves the source; writing a Note organizes the content I want to keep.** Saving something doesn't mean I've understood it, and it doesn't mean I have to create another Todo.

## Write decisions back when things change

Suppose I read the first draft, find it too broad, and say in Chat, "Let's leave automation out of this post." That shouldn't stay only in the conversation. The Todo's scope needs updating, the draft in Note needs editing, and the progress update needs to reflect the changes and the next step.

When I need to review a draft or make a decision, the Todo moves to `awaiting`: it's waiting for me. Work continues after my feedback, and we close it out once I confirm it's finished.

That connects the whole journey from idea to finished post: discuss it in Chat, create a Todo when I decide to follow through, preserve the sources of reference material, put the resulting content in Note, and feed feedback back into discussion and revision.

When I return a few days later, Todo tells me where things stand, and Note lets me get back to editing. I only revisit Chat when I want to trace a decision, rather than rereading the entire conversation every time.

## Separate, but connected

**Chat moves the work forward, Todo tracks it, and Note keeps the content.** Linking them makes it easier to go from an idea to a result, then build on that result in the next task.

Not everything needs the full workflow. A quick question can stay in Chat. A thought I want to keep can be just a Note. I create a Todo for work that needs ongoing attention, then link the relevant material to it.

I'm not trying to keep three copies of the same record. I'm keeping three different kinds of information: the discussion, the current state of the task, and content I can use again. Keeping each in its own place, and updating it there, means I don't have to reconstruct the whole task from a long conversation next time.
