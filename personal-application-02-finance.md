# Personal Application 02: Finance, Four Ways to Read It

This is the second Personal Application post. No code, just what the app handles and how information moves. The previous one was Todo, Chat, and Note. This one is my own Finance.

A company publishes a report every quarter and every year. With AI handling the bookkeeping, a person can produce something close to that: a report readable by quarter and by year. My Finance is that report. Its four tabs read one ledger.

Close to, not the same as. A company report is audited and follows a standard. Mine is not. The shape matches. The rigor does not.

At the end of a month I open it to answer a few questions: what I have now, what came in and what I spent, whether the investments made or lost money, which names the money sits in, and how far I am from enough. Buys, sells, and expenses all sit in one ledger, and the four tabs are four ways of reading it.

## Balance Sheet: what I have now

Balance Sheet is the balance sheet. It lays out assets and liabilities, then what is left after subtracting one from the other. The current view is the structure right now. The over-time view is how net worth moved by week, month, or year.

It answers what I have now, not what I spent this month. Assets are valued at the prices on the books. Which names the money sits in is read on this same sheet.

## Income Statement: what came in and went out

Income Statement is the income statement. For a chosen stretch, it totals income and expenses by account. It can also show each period by week, month, or year. Whatever the ledger recorded as income, and whatever it recorded as an expense, is here. If salary is on the books, it shows up as income.

It answers what was recorded coming in and going out. Which expenses count as living cost, and whether the investments made or lost money over the stretch, are read on this same sheet, not on a separate one.

## Transactions: back to one entry

Transactions is every entry in the ledger. I can find a buy, a sell, or an expense by name, then filter, sort, and page through. When a report does not add up, I come back here for the original entry, instead of editing the number on the report.

This page is for looking, not for editing. To change an entry, I change it in the ledger, and the other three tabs follow.

## FIRE: how far from the target

FIRE sets the net worth from the balance sheet against a target. The target is calculated from spending and a withdrawal rate. It is not a second set of assets. The page shows the gap, and the savings rate so far this year. It estimates a date only when savings are positive. Under that is net worth over time, with the target drawn as a line.

It uses both statements above. What I have now comes from the balance sheet. What came in and went out over the stretch is what the savings rate is built from. It does not decide whether to buy.

## Four readings, one ledger

**Balance Sheet is what I have now. Income Statement is what came in and went out. Transactions goes back to one entry. FIRE is the distance to a target.**

I don't walk all four every time. For a month, I usually start with Balance Sheet, then use Income Statement for that stretch. I open Transactions only when I need to check one entry. FIRE is the distance, not the daily book.

This post doesn't cover when to buy or when to sell. Those rules use the same numbers, but they are a different job. One level above the report, two things exist. AI-assisted investing is already in use: a monthly rebalance, checked against the buy and sell rules I have written down. Strategy backtesting is also a module, and it has not been put to real use yet. Neither is one of these four tabs, so this is as far as they go here.
